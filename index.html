Handpicked Destinations

Spiritual and Cultural Landmarks

Discover the 8 most iconic, sacred, and historically celebrated destinations that embody the soul of Kolhapur.

Spiritual Sanctum

Mahalakshmi Temple

Ancient Chalukya temple dedicated to Goddess Ambabai, constructed in 634 CE and revered as a sacred Shakti Peetha.

Explore Temple
Hilltop Pilgrimage

Jotiba Temple

Famous hilltop temple at 3,124 feet in Wadi Ratnagiri, dedicated to Lord Jyotiba with magnificent panoramic valley views.

Explore Temple
Royal Court

Bhavani Mandap

Historic court structure and administrative hub of the Maratha rulers during Chhatrapati Shahu Maharaj's golden reign.

Explore Mandap
Royal Heritage

New Palace

Magnificent black stone palace constructed in 1884, featuring Indo-Saracenic royal architecture and the Chhatrapati Shahu Museum.

Explore Palace
Cultural Museum

Kaneri Museum

Siddhagiri Gramjivan Museum, an open-air cultural sculpture park showcasing traditional Indian self-reliant village life.

Explore Museum
Maratha Fort

Panhala Fort

Largest hill fort in the Sahyadris, famous for Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj's historic escapes, Ambarkhana, and scenic bastions.

Explore Fort
Scenic Promenade

Rankala Lake

Historic freshwater lake with a picturesque evening promenade, vibrant local street food, boating, and Padmaraje Garden.

Explore Lake
Architectural Marvel

Shri Kopeshwar Temple

12th-century celestial stone temple on the banks of Krishna river in Khidrapur, celebrated for its circular open-air Swargamandap.

Explore Temple

About Kolhapur Kult

Explore smart. Travel deeper. Experience Kolhapur...

Kolhapur Kult is a modern digital platform built to redefine how travelers, historians, and explorers discover Kolhapur. We unite cultural heritage with modern web design to bring the city’s most celebrated treasures to your fingertips.

From thousand-year-old temple sanctums to towering Maratha hill forts and peaceful evening promenades, our mission is to make Kolhapur accessible, inspiring, and engaging for everyone.
